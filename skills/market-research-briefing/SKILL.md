# Market Research Briefing

Use for market scans, competitor research, account research, industry analysis, and opportunity discovery.

1. Define the decision the research must support, the market boundary, geography, time window, and key questions.
2. Prefer primary sources: company filings, official product/pricing pages, regulator publications, standards, public datasets, and direct statements.
3. Record source URL, publisher, publication/access date, and the exact claim supported.
4. Separate verified facts, company/vendor claims, third-party analysis, and inference.
5. Compare at least two independent sources for material claims where practical. Record conflicting evidence and source limitations.
6. Quantify only from traceable inputs. Show assumptions, formulas, units, and date ranges. Never invent TAM, revenue, pricing, customer counts, or market share.
7. Include competitor positioning, customer segments, unmet needs, risks, and open questions relevant to the decision.
8. Use public data only unless the workspace policy explicitly authorizes another data class.
9. End with a concise executive brief, evidence table, confidence level, and recommended next research action.

Do not present a research recommendation as a founder decision or commercial commitment.
