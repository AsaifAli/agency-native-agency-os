# Proposal and Statement-of-Work Drafting

Use when preparing a proposal, quotation narrative, statement of work, or change request.

1. Use only approved discovery records, service catalog entries, estimation inputs, and founder-authorized commercial terms.
2. Define the client problem, desired outcomes, proposed approach, deliverables, milestones, acceptance criteria, and responsibilities.
3. State assumptions, exclusions, dependencies, client obligations, risks, change-control rules, and support boundaries.
4. Use only approved prices, currency, validity periods, and payment terms. Mark missing inputs as unresolved; never invent them.
5. Distinguish estimates from commitments and identify what requires technical, legal, finance, or founder review.
6. Check scope traceability: every deliverable maps to a requirement and acceptance criterion.
7. Include a clear version, owner, date, and approval state.
8. Produce a draft only. Do not send, sign, accept, negotiate, or alter commercial commitments.
9. Escalate legal interpretation, unusual liability, regulatory claims, pricing exceptions, and contract terms to the founder and qualified advisers.

Output: a review-ready draft with assumptions, exclusions, open decisions, and approval checklist.
